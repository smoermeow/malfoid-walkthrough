# Global visual style

Retro anime, bold linework, Shirow meets Frieren. Mature proportions, detailed hand-drawn environments, expressive character acting, graphic silhouettes, selective perspective exaggeration, and strong anime impact frames only at key moments.

# Voice anchors

**Male 1:** Young male voice, clear and natural, slightly roughened by exhaustion, emotionally direct, with a grounded mid-range tone and restrained intensity.

**Malfoid:** Young female voice, cool and composed, smooth and slightly low-pitched, precise articulation, understated confidence, with a dry, lightly teasing edge rather than softness.

**Foideldore:** Mature female voice, grounded and conversational, slightly low and warm, with a subtle husky texture, clear and confident with natural authority, dry humor and emotional weight. Not aristocratic, theatrical, overly refined, or polished.

# Global lighting

Cold blue moonlight enters through the tall tower windows, contrasted by warm amber light from candles, lamps, and magical fixtures throughout Foideldore’s office. Strong rim light defines silhouettes around the windows while warm interior light shapes the characters and detailed hand-drawn environment.

# Plot summary

Male 1 and Malfoid stand before Foideldore after the events in the ruined courtyard. Male 1 argues that Malfoid cannot be sent to Azkaban after saving him, Male 2, and Female 1. Foideldore initially faces him near the windows, then turns away and remains silently looking outside while Male 1 continues arguing.

Malfoid becomes irritated by Male 1’s instinct to defend her. While watching him, however, her irritation unexpectedly gives way to attraction. She immediately rejects the thought, becomes embarrassed by her own reaction, and redirects that embarrassment into hostility toward Male 1.

Their argument escalates while Foideldore’s frustration builds in parallel. Foideldore finally turns, crosses back to the desk, and slams both hands down. After stopping their argument, she warns Male 1 that the Ministry may not be persuaded so easily, and that if they cannot be reasoned with, the situation may eventually move beyond her ability to prevent.
